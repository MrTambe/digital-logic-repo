\## The Master's Preface



I have watched computing evolve from the mechanical clatter of relays to the silent, lightning-fast dance of electrons in modern silicon. But the fundamental truth of it all has never changed: \*\*Computers do not do math; they do logic.\*\*



Welcome to the beginning of a very long journey. You are not here to learn how to write software; you are here to learn how to build the universe that software lives in. Over the course of these chapters—which will eventually span from this single page all the way to a full-fledged 64-bit architecture—you will learn to conjure intelligence out of literal sand.



We start with nothing. Two states: \*\*1 (True, High, On)\*\* and \*\*0 (False, Low, Off)\*\*. 



From these two states, we will build primitives. From primitives, we will build math. From math, we will build memory. And from memory and math combined, we will build a machine capable of autonomous execution: a computer. 



\### What You Will Achieve

By the end of Chapter 10, you will have constructed a fully functioning \*\*8-Bit Computer\*\* capable of executing a program from its own memory. But we will not stop there. The architecture you learn here scales. The exact same principles that govern our 8-bit calculator are the bedrock of the 64-bit processor powering the device you are reading this on. 



\### How to Read This Book

Modern learning is visual. Therefore, at the top of every chapter, you will find a live video demonstration of the circuit in action. Watch it first. Observe the flow of logic. Then, read the text below it. The text holds the deep theory—the \*why\* behind the \*what\*. If you only copy the videos, you will be a mechanic. If you read the theory, you will become an architect.



Let us begin.
