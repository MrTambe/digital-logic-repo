# 🛑 2.1 NOT Gate (Inverter)

The **NOT gate**, also known as an Inverter, is the simplest logic operation. It performs **Logical Negation**—meaning whatever signal enters the gate, the exact opposite signal comes out. 

If the input is ON (`1`), the output is OFF (`0`). If the input is OFF (`0`), the output is ON (`1`).

In Sebastian Lague's *Digital Logic Simulator*, the NOT gate is one of the two absolute foundational primitives (alongside the AND gate). You do not build this from smaller pieces; rather, every other component in this entire handbook is ultimately built using this block!

## 📐 Component Blueprint

Because the NOT gate is a base primitive provided directly by the simulator's bottom toolbar, there is no internal sub-circuit to wire. However, keeping track of its specifications is critical for calculating delay in larger, more complex chips later on.

* **Gate Depth (Propagation Delay):** `1 Tick`
* **Input Pins:** `1` (Data In)
* **Output Pins:** `1` (Inverted Data Out)
* **Dependencies:** None (Primitive Block)

### 📝 Design Notes
When optimizing complex circuits later (like ALUs or Adders), removing unnecessary NOT gates is the easiest way to speed up your processor. Two NOT gates placed in a row cancel each other out (an effect known as double negation: $ \overline{\overline{A}} = A $) and simply waste 2 ticks of clock cycle time without changing the data!
